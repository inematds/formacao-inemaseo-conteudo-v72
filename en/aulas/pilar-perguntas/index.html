Lesson 4 · 18 min

Pillar page and question pages with a direct answer

A pillar page is the long page that covers a central topic of your business. Around it, question pages each answer one question, starting with the answer. It's not an AI trick: it's respect for people who arrived with a question. And the questions section at the end stays useful to the reader, even without the showcase Google removed in 2026.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The difference between a pillar page and a question page
  2. 2The skeleton of a question page
  3. 3FAQ in 2026: useful to the reader, no showcase
  4. 4The swap test against generic text

Goal: write a question page with a direct answer at the top, sections shaped as questions, and a visible FAQ, without promising a rich result.

02 · Writing the question page

Tap each step.

1. Answer

Write the answer the way you'd tell a customer at the counter. Then cut whatever doesn't answer.

A short answer is clarity for the reader. No source shows an exact length as a factor for AI.

2. Prove it

Right after that, what only you have: the test you ran, your business's numbers, a customer's case (with permission), the mistake you made.

With a link to the evidence: repository, product page, course, photo.

3. Organize

List the follow-up questions the reader would ask and use them as section headings. The cluster variations go here.

Page title up to 60 characters and description between 110 and 160.

4. Close

Three or more real questions answered, if they exist. If they don't, no FAQ: making up questions is padding.

Links to the pillar page, 2 to 4 sister pages, and 2 catalog items (lesson 6).

At INEMAPillar pages 2 and 3 and the JARVIS question page were published on 09/24/2026; the pillar drafts were about 3,150 and 2,450 words.

03 · The skeleton, the request to the agent, and the swap test

The agent from your subscription writes the draft by reading your real material; you review it.

Question page skeleton
Title (≤ 60 characters): <question with the main keyword>
Description (110–160): <the answer in one sentence + what only you show>

<h1>Reader's question</h1>
<p>Direct answer in 2 to 4 sentences.</p>

<h2>How I know this: <your test/case></h2>
<p>First-hand data with a link to the evidence.</p>

<h2><reader's next question></h2>
<h2><what usually goes wrong?></h2>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>   (only if there are ≥ 3 real questions)

Also read: <pillar page> · <sister 1> · <sister 2>
Updated on <real date>
Draft request (agent)
Write the draft of a page that answers: "<question>".
ONLY source of facts: the files <paths to your real material>.
Rules:
- start with the answer in 2 to 4 sentences, no "in this article";
- a "How I know this" section with the first-hand data and the evidence link;
- section headings in question form;
- no number, name, or date that is not in the source; if something is missing, write [FALTA DADO];
- FAQ only with these real questions: <list>; without them, no FAQ.
At the end, list each number used and where it is in the source.
Swap test (CHECKLIST item 10)
For each paragraph:
1. swap the keyword for another of the same kind ("bread" → "cake", "law firm" → "accounting firm");
2. is the paragraph still true?
   yes → it's generic: rewrite it with your data;
   no → it's yours: it stays.
Example from the repository

The FAQ rich result stopped appearing on Google on 05/07/2026, and its documentation was removed on 06/12/2026. Anyone selling FAQPage as a showcase is selling the past.

Visible questions are still useful to readers. The markup, if you keep it, has to match the text on the page.

Google's AI features guide (May 2026) says there is no special optimization to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, no special schema, and that you can ignore content "chunking".

What it highlights is non-commodity content: your own point of view, direct experience. That's the "How I know this" section.

  • Rambling intro before the answer.
  • Made-up FAQ just to "have an FAQ".
  • A paragraph that passes the swap test: it fits any site, so it isn't yours.

04 · Practice: your first question page

Use a GO group from lesson 3.

E1 · Draft in the skeleton

60 min

Write (or ask the agent and review) a question page using the skeleton, with your first-hand data and the evidence link.

Done when: the answer is 2 to 4 sentences at the top, there is a section with your data and link, and no [FALTA DADO] was left unresolved.

E2 · Swap test

20 min

Apply the swap test to every paragraph and rewrite the generic ones.

Done when: you marked each paragraph as "yours" or "rewritten", and none stays true with the word swapped.

06 · Takeaways

Pillar page + questionsOne long pillar page per core topic; one page per question around it.
Answer, proof, questionsAnswer at the top, first-hand data with a link, sections phrased as questions.
Swap testIf the paragraph fits any site, rewrite it.
2026 yardstickA short answer is clarity, not an AI technique. The FAQ rich result ended on 05/07/2026; a visible FAQ is still useful to the reader.
