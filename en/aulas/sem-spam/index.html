Lesson 8 · 20 min

Scaled content and spam: what not to do

With AI, publishing a thousand pages costs almost nothing. That's exactly why Google targets scaled content: many pages made to manipulate rankings, "no matter how they are created." This lesson shows the shortcuts going around, why each one is a risk, and the right path: the 16-item checklist and a cadence that fits within human review.

02 · What the policy says, in four sentences

Click each one.

1. It's not the tool

Using AI is not prohibited. Google's guidance on AI-generated content accepts AI for researching and structuring original content that a person writes or validates.

2. Scale without value

The scaled content policy lists: lots of generated pages with no value, scraping results, stitching together content from other pages without adding anything, translating or swapping synonyms to disguise it, and creating several sites to hide the scale.

3. It applies to AI

Google clarified that the spam policies also apply to generative AI answers. And, since 04/13/2026, spam reports can be used for manual action.

4. Unrealistic promise

"First place in 60 seconds" is usually a keyword with no competition, cherry-picked proof, and an unstable result. Don't promise it, don't buy it.

03 · The 16-item checklist

From inemaSEO, adapted to the 2026 yardstick on items 1 and 7. Run each page through it before publishing.

CHECKLIST.md (adapted)
| # | Check | Blocks? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Direct answer at the start, no "in this article we will…" (clarity for the reader) | yes |
| 2 | ≥ 1 verifiable piece of first-hand data, with a link to the evidence | yes |
| 3 | No number or quote without a source; nothing made up by the AI | yes |
| 4 | Does not cannibalize: the main keyword does not belong to another page of yours | yes |
| 5 | Title 50–60 characters with the keyword; description 140–155; only one H1 | yes |
| 6 | Internal links: pillar page + ≥ 2 sibling pages + ≥ 2 catalog items; all 200 | yes |
| 7 | Valid JSON-LD that matches the visible text; FAQ only with ≥ 3 real questions, without expecting a rich result (ended on 05/07/2026) | yes |
| 8 | Real update date | yes |
| 9 | Human read: has an opinion and "what went wrong"; sounds like the author | yes |
| 10 | Swap test: swapped the keyword and the paragraph still holds? Rewrite it | yes |
| 11 | Cadence: ≤ 3 new pages per week in the same cluster | yes |
| 12 | Translation only with reciprocal hreflang | yes |
| 13 | Author block with a link to the author page (sameAs) | yes |
| 14 | Link ladder done: 2–3 older pages pointing to the new one, in the same commit | yes |
| 15 | Post-publication: URL 200, title, canonical and JSON-LD present; search engines notified | yes |
| 16 | Own images and diagrams when they help (not stock images) | no |

Example from the repository

The original checklist asks for an answer in the first 60 words "in the format AI search engines cite" and FAQ markup for the rich result. By the 2026 yardstick: a short answer is clarity, with no source backing it up as an AI factor, and the FAQ rich result stopped appearing on 05/07/2026.

From the method's Plan B: a general drop after publishing a lot is a sign of scaled content. The response is to stop, audit with the checklist, merge or remove the weak pages, and lower the cadence.
