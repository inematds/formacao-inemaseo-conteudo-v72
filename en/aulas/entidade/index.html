Lesson 1 · 16 min

Entity: telling search engines who is behind the site

Content only you can write starts by saying who "you" are. Google and AI tools gather pages, profiles, and mentions into an entity: a person, a brand. If the site doesn't declare this in one single way, each page looks like it was written by a different author, and your name can even be confused with someone else's.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What an entity is and why it comes before content
  2. 2The JSON-LD template with author and brand under a fixed @id
  3. 3How article, news, and course point to the same entity
  4. 4Testing whether AI tools confuse your name with another

Goal: declare a Person and an Organization with a fixed @id and a separate sameAs, and make the rest of the site point to them.

01 · One entity, many pages pointing to it

Tap each block.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Declare once, point always: the rest of the course writes content on behalf of this entity.

02 · Build the entity in four steps

Tap each step.

1. Choose the @id values

Use the canonical domain from course 1 and a fragment: #autor and #organizacao. Don't change them again.

If you have subdomains (news, events), they all use exactly the same @id values as the main site.

2. Fill in the template

Name, "about" page, logo, and the official profiles. Only profiles that exist and that you control.

The "about" page must say, in visible text, the same thing the markup says.

3. Put it on every page

The entity block goes into the layout, the same on every page.

The markup on each page (article, news, course) points to it by @id, without repeating data.

On inema.clubOn 09/24/2026, Person #nei and Organization #organization started appearing on every page of the portal (commit 965d901).

4. Test the name

Ask two assistants "what is [your brand]?" If the answer talks about another company or agency, you have a name collision.

Markup alone doesn't solve a collision: it takes pages that clearly say who you are, and consistent profiles.

03 · The entity template and a page pointing to it

Swap in your domain, names, and profiles. Don't invent a profile that doesn't exist.

jsonld-entidade.json (inemaSEO templates)
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    {
      "@type": "Person",
      "@id": "https://www.seusite.com/#autor",
      "name": "Seu Nome",
      "url": "https://www.seusite.com/sobre/",
      "worksFor": { "@id": "https://www.seusite.com/#organizacao" },
      "sameAs": ["https://github.com/seu-usuario", "https://www.linkedin.com/in/seu-perfil/"]
    },
    {
      "@type": "Organization",
      "@id": "https://www.seusite.com/#organizacao",
      "name": "Sua Marca",
      "url": "https://www.seusite.com/",
      "logo": "https://www.seusite.com/logo.png",
      "founder": { "@id": "https://www.seusite.com/#autor" },
      "sameAs": ["https://www.youtube.com/@suamarca", "https://www.instagram.com/suamarca"]
    }
  ]
}
Article pointing to the entity
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Article",
  "headline": "Título igual ao da página",
  "datePublished": "2026-10-19",
  "dateModified": "2026-10-19",
  "author": { "@id": "https://www.seusite.com/#autor" },

}

Example from the repository

JSON-LD from the inema.club home page, only the items with @id:

EducationalOrganization  https://www.inema.club/#organization  sameAs: 5
Person                   https://www.inema.club/#nei           sameAs: 1
WebSite                  https://www.inema.club/#website

On a question page of the same site:

publisher → {"@id": "https://www.inema.club/#organization"}

On 07/10/2026, INEMA's fixed battery of 20 questions ran on Claude and Gemini. INEMA showed up in 0 of 48 neutral answers on Claude and in 0 of 4 on Gemini.

Worse: "What is INEMA?" got 4 of 4 answers about INEMA-BA, an environmental agency. After that, 54 listing pages were published under /conhecimento/ saying who INEMA is.

  • One @id per page: it becomes a new entity on every page.
  • Mixed-up sameAs: the person's profile on the brand, and vice versa.
  • Markup the text doesn't confirm: Google asks that the structure match what's visible.

04 · Practice: your entity

Use the canonical domain you chose in course 1.

E1 · Entity published

30 min

Fill in the template with your data, put it in the site layout, and make at least one page type (article, product, or course) point to the @id. Publish and run the X-ray on 3 pages.

Done when: the schema.org validator shows Person and Organization with no errors, and the X-ray shows both on all 3 pages.

E2 · Collision test

15 min

Ask "what is [your brand]?" and "who is [your name]?" on two assistants. Fill in the notes.

Done when: the notes say, for each assistant, whether it talked about you, about someone else (who), or didn't know, and what you will write on the "about" page.

05 · Test what you learned

The explanation shows up after your answer.

Question 1 of 4

The news site is a subdomain of the main site. Which @id does it use for the organization?skill

Question 2 of 4

Where do the personal LinkedIn and the brand's Instagram profiles go?

Question 3 of 4

"What is [your brand]?" comes back talking about a public agency with the same name. What is this?

Question 4 of 4

The markup says the author is an expert with 20 years of experience, but the page doesn't mention it. Is that okay?

06 · Takeaways

Two fixed @id valuesOne author and one brand, the same on every page and subdomain.
Point, don't repeatArticle, news, and course cite the @id.
Test the nameA name collision shows up when you ask the question and write down the answer.
2026 yardstickThere is no special schema for AI: an entity matters because it says who is behind the content and matches the text.
