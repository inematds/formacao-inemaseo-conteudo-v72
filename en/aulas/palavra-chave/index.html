Lesson 3 · 18 min

Validate a keyword without a paid tool

Writing about what nobody searches for is wasted work; so is writing about what every big portal already owns. Before you open the editor, score each keyword with free signals: autocomplete, Google Trends, Search Console, and the search results themselves. And only move forward if you have something real to say.

In this lesson you will

  2. 2The GO / MAYBE / NO-GO score, criterion by criterion
  3. 3Group keywords by intent in a spreadsheet

Without first-hand data, the score drops three points: that's how the score separates your content from non-commodity content.

02 · Score a keyword in five looks

Tap each step.

1. Autocomplete

Type the start of the search into Google, in an incognito window. If the phrase shows up as a suggestion, someone searches for it.

Repeat with letters after the term ("how to make bread a", "b"...) to collect real variations.

2. Trends

In Google Trends, compare with an anchor term you know. Interest above zero over 12 months: +2. At least 10% of the anchor: +3.

Zero over 12 months is a NO-GO signal, no matter how much you like the idea.

3. Search Console

If the query already has an impression in your Search Console, at any position, it earns the highest point: it's confirmed demand for your site.

A query with impressions and no dedicated page is a content-plan topic validated for free (course 3 goes deeper).

4. Search result

Open the top 10. At least 3 weak results (forum, video without text, old page, another language): +2.

Top 10 dominated by big platforms with a page specific to the search: −2. Intent to learn or buy: +1.

03 · The score spreadsheet and the grouping request

Copy it into your spreadsheet. The grouping can be done by an agent on your subscription, with your review.

GO/NO-GO score (inemaSEO method, step 6)
| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Shows up in Google autocomplete | +2 |
| Google Trends > 0 over 12 months (≥ 10% of an anchor term: +3) | +2 |
| Already has impressions in Search Console | +3 |
| Intent to learn or buy | +1 |
| Top 10 with ≥ 3 weak results (forum, video with no text, old, other language) | +2 |
| Top 10 dominated by big platforms with a specific page | −2 |
| You have a real experiment/course/project to cite | +2 (without it: −3) |

GO ≥ 6 · MAYBE 3–5 · NO-GO ≤ 2

Simulation: illustrative example

Fictional example, from a sourdough bakery:

  • "como fazer levain": autocomplete +2, Trends +3, no impressions 0, learning +1, top 10 with 3 weak results +2, recipe tested at the bakery itself +2 → 10, GO.
  • "melhor pão do Brasil": autocomplete +2, Trends +2, no impressions 0, no clear intent 0, big portals −2, nothing first-hand −3 → −1, NO-GO.

Exact search volume doesn't exist for free. Google Ads Keyword Planner, in an account with no campaign, shows only ranges (10–100, 100–1K).

Paid tools (DataForSEO, Semrush, Ahrefs) give numbers and difficulty. They're optional: check the price on each site. The score above decides without them.

04 · Practice: your content plan

A spreadsheet with the template's columns.

E1 · Ten scored terms

60 min

Build a list of 10 terms from your topic and score each one with the five checks. In the "dado próprio" column, write which experiment, product, or real case you would cite.

Done when: the spreadsheet has 10 rows with a score and a decision, and every GO row has its first-hand data described.

E2 · Clusters

20 min

Group the GO terms by intent (by hand or with the request to the agent) and check the result. Cross-check with the canonical map from lesson 2.

Done when: each group has one main keyword and one owner page, and no group produced two pages.
